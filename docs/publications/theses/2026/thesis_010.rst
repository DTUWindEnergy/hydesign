.. thesis_010:

Techno-economic optimization of hybrid PV-Wind-BESS facilities and grid integration in DK1
==========================================================================================================================

**Abstract**

The decarbonisation of electricity systems under the Paris Agreement has made wind and
solar photovoltaics (PV) the central instruments of the energy transition, yet their rapid expansion
creates a structural market value problem. As variable renewable energy (VRE)
capacity grows, generators produce simultaneously during highoutput
periods and systematically plummets spot prices in the very hours they earn most of their revenue. Colocated
Hybrid power plants (HPPs) that incorporates wind, solar PV, and battery energy
storage (BESS) behind a shared grid connection offer a response, exploiting the temporal
synergy of wind and solar generation and using storage to shift output toward higher
price time windows through arbitrage, and reduce curtailment through peak shaving. The
optimal sequencing of capital investment across such systems, however, remains underexplored,
particularly the choice between hybridising an existing wind plant and pursuing greenfield PVBESS
development while preserving the option to add wind in the future.
This thesis develops a technoeconomic optimisation framework, built on a structurally
modified version of the HyDesign tool, to optimise HPP component sizing and configuration under physical, landuse,
and interconnection constraints. The framework is applied
to two real case studies provided by the Danish energy company NRGi in the DK1 bidding
zone: the Tuekær colocated PVWindBESS project and the Dalsgård PVBESS project.
For Tuekær, the optimal design installs 62 MW of PV across the full 70hectare
site in Phase 1, followed by a 133 MW expansion in year 4. For Dalsgård, 34 MW of PV in Phase
1 with a marginal 2 MW addition in year 13. Under current cost and market conditions,
BESS is excluded from both optimal solutions, as arbitrage and curtailmentmitigation
revenues fail to offset its capital cost. Adding PV to a standalone wind asset yields substantially
greater profitability gains than adding BESS, and brownfield wind hybridisation
outperforms the reversed greenfieldfirst timeline, since early BESS revenue and deferred
turbine costs cannot offset the nearterm revenue of operating wind from project inception.
Finally, longterm power purchase agreements (PPAs) materially derisk financial
outcomes against weather variability without altering the spotoptimised physical configuration,
indicating that developers should size assets on spotmarket fundamentals and secure PPAs against that baseline.
